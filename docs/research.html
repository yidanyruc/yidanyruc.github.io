Working papers

Inconvenient Liquidity

with Guillaume Rocheteau

This paper develops a theory of positive and negative liquidity premia. Liquid assets facilitate decentralized exchange, but their marginal nonpecuniary value declines as they become more abundant and turns negative above a threshold—liquidity becomes inconvenient at the margin despite its positive social value. With multiple assets, positive and negative liquidity premia can coexist. Equilibrium exhibits ex post portfolio heterogeneity, with liquidity-constrained and unconstrained agents coexisting endogenously. A quantitative exercise generates liquidity premia of empirically plausible magnitudes and signs for long-maturity U.S. Treasuries. Finally, the socially optimal real return on money exceeds the Friedman-rule rate.

Market Power in Capital Markets: Misallocation and Growth

with Michael Choi and Guillaume Rocheteau

This paper develops a general equilibrium model in which firms are heterogeneous and experience idiosyncratic productivity shocks that require capital reallocation through a frictional dealer market. The market power of dealers is endogenous: dealers can enhance their bargaining power by exerting costly effort. We examine the implications for capital misallocation and growth. The model admits multiple balanced-growth paths, each associated with distinct real interest rates, growth rates, and TFP levels. We investigate the role of endogenous bargaining power in the presence of a learning-by-doing externality. We conclude by offering a reinterpretation in terms of information acquisition and price discrimination.

CBDC with Smart Contracts and Banking

with Kai Liu and Yu Zhu

This paper studies the impact of introducing a central bank digital currency (CBDC) with smart contracts as a restricted lending method on bank lending to micro-, small-, and medium-sized enterprises (MSMEs). It highlights an important trade-off: while the CBDC can help the bank screen enterprises, reduce loans to low-productivity enterprises, and improve loan quality, it may also limit credit creation and decrease lending to high-productivity enterprises. The key friction in the model is asymmetric information between enterprises and the bank. The findings show that CBDCs can significantly enhance MSME financing, but may decrease social welfare in some cases. The paper derives conditions under which CBDCs may either increase or decrease social welfare. It also shows that the disintermediation caused by CBDCs is not necessarily detrimental, as it can help MSMEs obtain loans, increase bank profits, and potentially improve social welfare.

Borrowing to Bargain

with Tai-Wei Hu and Guillaume Rocheteau

We develop an equilibrium model of an economy with centralized and decentralized markets in which ex ante identical agents with full commitment and sufficient resources in each period to finance the efficient allocation nevertheless optimally borrow intertemporally in centralized markets so that debt repayment leaves them constrained in decentralized markets. By becoming constrained, borrowers increase their effective bargaining strength and private surplus. Because they fail to internalize the reduction in surplus imposed on their future trading partners, equilibria feature overborrowing. Unlike existing theories, overborrowing does not rely on collateral constraints, default risk, fire-sale externalities, limited liability, or limited commitment.
